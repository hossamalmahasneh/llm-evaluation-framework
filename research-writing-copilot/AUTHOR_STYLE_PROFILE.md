# Author Writing Profile — Hossam Almahasneh (v1)
Source: six user-supplied coauthored pre-LLM academic manuscripts. This profile describes recurring observable manuscript features; it does not prove sole authorship of any passage. Use as guidance, not as an AI-detector avoidance mechanism.

## Strict mode
- Preserve the author's research question, actual methodology, evidence, numerical results, limitations, and intended argument. Do not add hypotheses, results, citations, or unsupported novelty claims.
- Match recurring discourse sequence: practical/scientific problem → prior measures or techniques → gap/technical difficulty → specific proposed approach → experiment design → measured results → restrained interpretation.
- Use direct technical exposition, with definitions of acronyms at first mention, specific instrument/procedure descriptions, and references to figures, tables, equations and sections only when supplied.
- Prefer concrete statements about what was investigated, measured, extracted, compared, observed, and suggested. Results are followed by interpretation linked to the experiment.
- Keep paragraphs organized around one technical function. IEEE-style short paper sections can be concise; longer journal manuscripts use fuller literature review and detailed experimental description.
- Transitional patterns recurring in samples include: In this study; In addition; Moreover; Therefore; However; On the other hand; As shown in Fig.; Results show/suggest; The remainder of this paper. Use only where logically justified; do not mechanically repeat.
- Preserve source-specific technical terms (EEG, cognitive distraction, SVD, r-principal subspace, frontal cortex, coherence) only when relevant to the NEW study. Do not import domain vocabulary into unrelated subjects.
- Scientific caution: use “suggests”, “may”, or “indicates” when the underlying evidence is preliminary; distinguish observation from inference. Avoid stronger causal language than the study supports.
- Citations must use the author's supplied bibliography. Never invent sources or imply a source supports a claim without supplied evidence.
- Do not intentionally reproduce typographical, grammatical, layout or OCR artifacts; these are not a reliable signature of authorial style. Do not copy passages from the examples into a new manuscript.
- Never imply that AI assistance was absent. No detector score or undetectability guarantee.

## Source distinctions
- The first-author Cognitive Psychology manuscript gives a longer introduction, experimental narrative and results with statistical tests.
- The IEEE conference papers favor compact abstract, introduction, apparatus, signal processing and results organization.
- The r-largest singular-value manuscript includes formal mathematical exposition and more detailed methodological derivation.
- All six are coauthored and overlap in research topic and dataset; do not overstate independence or infer a universal individual style from them.

## Drafting procedure
1. Collect author-supplied original claims, methods, verified results, references and target journal.
2. Draft one section using this profile as style guidance, grounding each factual statement.
3. Flag gaps as [EVIDENCE NEEDED]; never fill them speculatively.
4. Offer the author the draft for explicit approval; retain revision history.
